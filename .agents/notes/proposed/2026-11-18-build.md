# Agent Note: Build

Status: proposed — a third kind of fleet beside Task and Duty: the lead plans, the person approves the plan, and the fleet then builds it in parallel inside the limits the plan set

Related: [drafting a project](../implemented/2026-11-13-drafting-a-project.md), which is
the same step one level up; [standing teams](2026-11-12-standing-teams.md);
[short-lived workers](2026-11-14-short-lived-workers.md);
[shared team memory](2026-11-12-shared-team-memory.md).

## Problem

A fleet is a Task or a Duty, and neither fits work that is large and not yet specified.

- **A task** suits what is small or precisely described. Given something open ("add
  what KFM needs next"), the lead decides what to build and builds it.
- **A duty** is hands-off by design. It can spend on work nobody wanted and get it
  wrong, and the person finds out afterwards.

In the run of 2026-11-14 a lead chose two features by itself, three workers spent $3.03
in nine minutes building them, one crashed on every real use, and the person had both
taken out. A page describing them would have cost cents and been refused before
anything was built.

What is missing is the middle: the person decides what is built and what it may cost,
once, and is then left alone while many agents do it quickly.

## Proposal

A fleet has one of three kinds, chosen where Task and Duty are chosen today (the
person, 2026-11-18):

| | For | The person's part |
|---|---|---|
| Task | small or precisely specified work | says what to do |
| Duty | a role kept going, hands off | stops it when it goes wrong |
| Build | large work done fast by many agents | approves the plan, then is left alone |

A Build has two phases, and what the lead can do in each is set by KFM, not asked of it.

### Planning

- The lead is opened able to read and search the work folder and to write nothing: its
  permissions are read-only, and what it asks approval for is refused, as for the agent
  that drafts a project.
- It has one fleet tool, `plan`, in place of `delegate`. The plan arrives as the tool's
  arguments, so its shape is checked and nothing is read out of prose:
  - what will be built, in a few sentences;
  - the parts, each with a name, what it is, what done means for it, the rank of the
    agent that does it, and the most it may spend.
- The parts' limits may not add up to more than the fleet's limit less what planning
  cost. A plan that does is refused with that fact.
- The fleet then waits. Its status is "plan to approve".

### The person's decision

The plan is a page of the window, like a project's draft: the summary, a row for each
part with its limit, and the total beside the fleet's limit. The person can change a
part's limit, leave a part out, approve, or send it back with a note, which the lead
gets as a message and answers with a new plan. In the terminal `kfm plan` shows it and
`kfm plan approve` or `kfm plan revise "note"` answers it.

### Building

On approval KFM itself adds one agent for each part under the lead, with the part as
its task, the plan's summary as what it is part of, and the part's limit as its own.
They start together. The lead gets its usual permissions and tools back, with two
differences:

- `delegate` is refused with the fact that this fleet works to an approved plan.
- `plan` proposes a change (a part added, a limit raised), which goes to the person as
  a card and applies when approved.

A part that reaches its limit stops and its agent's supervisor is told, as today. The
fleet ends when the lead reports, as a task does.

**Nothing tells the lead how to plan or the workers how to build.** What controls a
Build is what each agent is able to do in each phase and what each part may spend.

## Alternatives considered

- **"Plan first" as a switch on a task.** The first form of this idea. It makes the plan
  a courtesy before ordinary delegation; the person's view (2026-11-18) is that the plan
  is what releases the money, which needs parts and limits KFM can hold a fleet to.
- **Telling leads to plan and ask before building.** A rule, applied also where it makes
  no sense (a typo, a support team), and one a lead can follow badly. See
  [short-lived workers](2026-11-14-short-lived-workers.md) on rules for agents.
- **Kadence's `propose` tool**, with which an agent puts one action of its own to the
  user. It asks about each action; a Build asks once about all of them.
- **A fleet that only proposes**, with the building done by a second fleet the person
  starts. The lead that read the project and wrote the plan would be thrown away, and
  the second fleet's lead would start cold.

## Acceptance criteria

- Started on open-ended work, a Build spends only the lead's reading before the person
  has approved anything, and changes no file.
- After approval the fleet's agents are the plan's parts, each stopped by its own limit,
  and the fleet never spends more than the plan's total plus what planning cost.
- A lead that wants a part the plan lacks reaches the person, not `delegate`.
- The same open-ended instruction given to a Build and to a Task: the Build's result is
  what the person approved; measured in what was built that the person then keeps.

## Risks

- **A plan can read well and build badly.** It prevents unwanted work, not wrong work.
  Each part's agent runs the project's tests, and checking the parts together is left
  to the lead, which is weakest exactly when many parts report at once.
- **Every part starts cold.** Ten parts read the project ten times. The plan's summary
  helps; [shared team memory](2026-11-12-shared-team-memory.md) is the real answer and
  is not built.
- **Parts that depend on each other.** Agents started together may need each other's
  results. The plan has no order; the lead is where they meet.
- **One more place a fleet waits for the person.** That is the point of it, and a Build
  nobody looks at does nothing.
- **Fixed parts may be too rigid.** A lead that cannot delegate cannot react to what
  building turns up without asking. If changes are asked for constantly, the plan is at
  the wrong level.
